# Curriculum and coverage matrix

## Scope, stated honestly

**What "finished" means.** Finishing the course means *broad working competence* across the
core of system design: you can scope a problem, estimate it, choose and justify components,
predict how they behave under load and failure, and diagnose common production problems.
Concretely, every concept below has reached at least *Demonstrated* (two independent successes
on different items, one not multiple choice), most have reached *Retained* (success after a
gap of at least 7 days), and you have passed the cumulative and final assessments and worked
all ten case studies, unguided variants included. The rules for those states are in
[LEARNING_DESIGN.md](LEARNING_DESIGN.md).

**What it does not mean.**

- It does not cover everything. It covers about 110 core topics in enough depth to reason
  with. Each topic is deep enough for interviews and for reading the primary sources
  confidently; specialist depth is left to those sources.
- Quizzes are not engineering experience. Recall and scenario questions train judgement, but
  operating a real system, being paged, and living with your own design decisions teach
  things this app cannot. The six labs are the closest substitute. They are optional and need
  a computer, and they are where the material becomes real.
- Free-text answers are self-graded against rubrics, and self-grading is not proven accurate
  (see LEARNING_DESIGN.md). Treat a passed rubric as "my answer had these reasoning steps",
  not as expert review.

**Deliberately out of scope.**

- Cryptographic algorithm internals, formal verification, and proofs (FLP, CAP and Paxos are
  covered at the level of statement and intuition).
- Byzantine fault-tolerant protocols and blockchains (Byzantine faults are introduced only as
  a system-model assumption).
- Networking below the transport layer (routing, Ethernet, wireless); IP is assumed.
- Specific cloud products as subjects in their own right. Vendor docs are cited only where
  they are the clearest primary description of a general mechanism.
- Machine-learning systems, ranking and recommendation algorithms (feeds and search treat
  ranking as a black box).
- Front-end and mobile client architecture; Kubernetes and infrastructure-as-code operations.
- Organisational practice: on-call rotations, incident command and postmortem culture appear
  only as pointers.
- Multi-region active-active design is touched on (replication, CAP, invalidation) but has no
  dedicated lesson.

## How to read the tables

- **Prerequisites** are lessons outside the row that the row's lessons depend on.
- **Exercise types** use the item formats and skills from [CONTENT_GUIDE.md](CONTENT_GUIDE.md).
  Every lesson has at least 4 practice items and 2 assessment items in at least 3 formats.
- **Assessed by**: *topic quiz* = the assessment pool right after the module section;
  *delayed check* = spaced re-tests of each concept on later days; *cumulative* = interleaved
  quizzes across every module studied so far; *final* = held-out scenarios in
  `content/items/final-*.yaml`. Cases and labs are named where they exercise the topic.
- Source keys refer to `content/sources/core.yaml`; per-source notes are in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).
